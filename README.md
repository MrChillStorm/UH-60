# UH-60A Black Hawk for FlightGear, with a JSBSim model built from the GenHel data

The Sikorsky UH-60A Black Hawk from FlightGear's aircraft collection, with a
new JSBSim flight model in place of its YASim one. The model follows the
engineering simulation Sikorsky wrote for the Black Hawk (GenHel, documented
for NASA in CR-166309), and is checked against what the real aircraft is
documented to do:

- the US Army's flight tests (USAAEFA projects 77-17, 79-24 and 83-24):
  trims from hover to 162 kt, hover and cruise power, sideslip and bank,
  and control steps with the stability augmentation off,
- the operator's manual (TM 1-1520-237-10), for ground effect, warning
  lights and procedures,
- NASA's own validation of GenHel (TM-88360, TM-85890).

How it was built, and the comparisons test by test, are in the report:
[UH60A-JSBSim-model-report.pdf](UH60A-JSBSim-model-report.pdf).

## What's simulated

- **Main rotor.** The four blades are simulated one by one, each with its
  own flapping and lead-lag, the lag damper, and eight aerodynamic
  segments using the SC1095 airfoil tables at their own angle of attack
  and Mach number, in reverse flow too. The induced flow is a dynamic
  wake (Peters–He), with ground effect from the hover chart and its fade
  in forward flight. Retreating blade stall, vortex ring state and
  translational lift come out of this, not out of special cases.
- **Tail rotor** after GenHel, canted 20° so that it also lifts, with the
  main rotor's wake and the fin's blockage.
- **Fuselage, stabilator and fin** from GenHel's wind tunnel tables, in the
  main rotor's wake.
- **Engines and rotor speed.** Two T700 engines with their fuel controls,
  start sequence and power turbine governors; the rotor speed is free, so
  it droops when you pull hard and the engines catch it, and you can
  autorotate.
- **Flight controls as in the aircraft:** the mechanical mixing, the
  stabilator schedule, the two stability augmentation channels (SAS), the
  pitch bias actuator and the flight path stabilization (FPS) with
  attitude, airspeed and heading hold and turn coordination.

All of it is JSBSim XML, generated by a script from the transcribed data;
no Nasal runs the flight model.

## Install

Get the files:

```bash
git clone https://github.com/MrChillStorm/UH-60.git
```

Then add the cloned `UH-60` folder in the FlightGear launcher under Add-ons →
Additional aircraft folders (or start FlightGear with `--fg-aircraft=`
pointing at it), and pick **Sikorsky UH-60A Black Hawk**.

- Tested with FlightGear 2024.1.
- The aircraft is in the `UH-60A` folder with the id `uh60a`, so it sits next
  to the UH-60 from the hangar without replacing it.
- In multiplayer, other pilots need this aircraft installed to see yours as a
  UH-60A.

## Flying it

- **Start.** On the ground the aircraft starts cold and dark (in the air it
  starts running). Aircraft menu → AutoStart/AutoStop, or the `}` key, runs
  the start: battery and generators on, one engine after the other, then
  both to FLY; again, it shuts down. To start by hand, the power control
  levers are the mixture axis or keys (0 OFF, 0.5 IDLE, 1 FLY) and the
  starter is `s` for the selected engine. An engine lights off at about
  20 % Ng, the starter drops out at 52–65 % and it idles at 63–69 %. There
  is no cockpit battery switch: set `/controls/electric/battery-switch`, or
  let the autostart do it.
- **Collective** is the throttle axis, the way round of the original
  aircraft: full throttle is the collective's bottom. Reverse your throttle
  axis if you want it the other way.
- **Flight controls dialog** (Aircraft menu → Flight controls): SAS 1 and 2,
  FPS, pitch bias actuator and the stabilator's automatic mode, with an
  "All AFCS on" button. A message on screen tells you when any of them is
  off. With all of it on, the aircraft holds its attitude hands-off; without
  the SAS, a hover takes your full attention, as in the real aircraft.
  FlightGear can't move your stick, so the FPS moves the stick's trim point
  instead.
- **Warning lights.** The master warning panel has ENG OUT (Ng below 55 %),
  LOW ROTOR RPM (below 96 %, flashing), MASTER CAUTION and a green light
  when the whole AFCS is on. The caution/advisory panel lights the capsules
  the simulation can drive, as the operator's manual describes them: fuel
  low and fuel pressure, engine oil, starters, generators and converters
  (off line below 93.75 % rotor speed on the ground), hydraulic pumps and
  servos, stabilator, SAS and FPS, transmission oil, prime pump, landing
  light and parking brake. MASTER CAUTION comes on with each new amber
  capsule; click it to reset. Hold the panel's BRT/DIM-TEST switch down to
  test every light.
- **Engine ignition switch** below the caution/advisory panel: click to turn
  it; the engines only light off with it ON.
- **Feel.** The rotor's wake is turbulent near the ground, through
  translational lift and in vortex ring; the cockpit shakes with the rotor's
  vibration, more at high speed and a lot in blade stall. The wake's
  turbulence can be scaled with `/fdm/jsbsim/rotor/turb/scale`.
- **Keys:** `d`/`D` crew and passenger doors, `e`/`E` and `f`/`F` windows and
  bays, `R` rotor brake on/off, `r` rotor brake while held, `Q` reset view.

## How it compares

Every number comes from the scripted test programme in the report. GenHel is
Sikorsky's simulation as NASA ran it.

| | Reference | This model |
|---|---|---|
| Level-flight trim, 50–162 kt | USAAEFA (TM-88360 fig. B1) | longitudinal cyclic and collective within 7.1 % of travel, pitch within 1.9° |
| Bank and lateral cyclic at zero sideslip, 60–140 kt | USAAEFA 79-24 | within 1.4° and 0.31 in |
| Hover power out of ground effect, 13,000–19,000 lb | USAAEFA tethered hover | 0.965–0.995 of the test |
| Ground effect, 5–40 ft wheel height | operator's manual | within 0.006 of the torque ratio |
| Rotor figure of merit | full-scale and model rotor tests | within 0.026 |
| Main rotor power in cruise, 72–162 kt | USAAEFA 79-24 | within 3.6 % |
| Control steps, SAS off: rms error of the rate | USAAEFA 79-24 | 2.07 deg/s (GenHel 2.77), better than GenHel in 11 of 16 cases |
| Pitch rate after a 1 in aft step at 100 kt | 24.1 deg/s | 17.4 (GenHel 19.3) |

## Known gaps

- **Pitch at speed** is too stable: the pitch-up after an aft cyclic step at
  100 kt is weaker than in the test, and the slow pitch divergence the Army
  measured with the SAS on doesn't appear. The model's angle-of-attack
  stability derivative is a third of GenHel's.
- **Cruise power** is 2–10 % above the 77-17 test, which disagrees by 4–6 %
  with the 79-24 test of the same airframe; the model sits between them.
- **Lateral-directional:** at 100 kt a pedal step rolls the model (and
  GenHel) the wrong way; the bank changes half as much between climb and
  descent as in the test; lateral cyclic moves the aircraft about 1.5 times
  as much per inch as the test suggests.
- **Tail rotor** hover power is 5–11 % low.
- **Trim at 162 kt:** the lateral cyclic is 10 % and the pedal 7 % away from
  the test.
- **Not modelled:** blade torsion and dynamic stall (heavy and fast, the
  retreating blade stalls early instead), rotor damage and overstress,
  hydraulic and most electrical failures. The transmission oil gauges are
  cosmetic.
- **Cockpit:** the mode selectors, the auxiliary fuel management panel, the
  radar warning indicator, the ice rate meter and the blade deice and IRCM
  panels are there but drive nothing. The caution capsules for failures the
  simulation doesn't have stay dark, and the hydraulic ones are lit below
  half rotor speed, an estimate.

## Rebuilding the model and the report

The [`fdm`](fdm) folder holds everything that produced the flight model and
the report. It needs Python 3 with `jsbsim` 1.3.2, `numpy` and `matplotlib`
(`scipy` for `heyson.py`), and [tectonic](https://tectonic-typesetting.github.io)
or another XeLaTeX for the PDF.

```bash
cd fdm
python3 genuh60.py ../UH-60A --fg
python3 genuh60.py root/aircraft/uh60a
python3 genap.py
```

- `genuh60.py` writes the flight model: `--fg` into the aircraft folder
  (`uh60a.xml` and `Systems/uh60a-*.xml`), otherwise test versions for the
  scripts below into `root/`. `engines.py` writes the engines and drive train,
  `uh60data.py` holds the data with their sources, and `data/` the
  transcribed tables: GenHel's airfoil, fuselage and tail data, the
  digitised flight tests, and the ground effect ratios of NASA TN D-234
  (which `heyson.py` recomputes).
- `genap.py` writes the test autopilot (`Systems/uh60a-autopilot*.xml`), which
  flew the FlightGear flight tests. It holds hover height, position, heading,
  altitude and airspeed through `/autopilot/uh60/*`; it is a test tool, not a
  UH-60A system.
- `harness.py` drives the model offline. `sweep.py` (level-flight trims),
  `dynamics.py` (control steps), `latdir.py` (sideslip and bank) and
  `performance.py` (hover and cruise power) compare it with the flight tests.
- `report/mkresults.py` runs the whole programme (a few minutes on 8 cores)
  into `report/results/`; `report/build.sh` makes every figure, table and
  number of the report from them and builds the PDF.
- `cockpit/panel_layout.py` measures the instrument panel from the
  operator's manual's figure (`cockpit/panel-layout.md` and `.png`), and
  `cockpit/panel_face.py` builds the cockpit panel from it: the face plate,
  its texture and the instruments' places. `cockpit/instruments.py` (with
  `cap.py`) builds the instruments the old panel lacked. Both draw their
  textures from the manual's PDF:
  `python3 instruments.py TM-1-1520-237-10.pdf`, then
  `python3 panel_face.py TM-1-1520-237-10.pdf`.

The NASA and Army reports are not included; the report lists them as sources.

## Changes to the FlightGear aircraft

Based on the UH-60 for FlightGear by its original author (aircraft version
1.1). Changed in 2026, all inside `UH-60A`:

- **Folder and id.** Renamed from `UH-60` to `UH-60A` and from `uh60-set.xml`
  to `uh60a-set.xml`; the `Aircraft/UH-60/` paths inside the files point to
  the new name.
- **Flight model.** `Systems/uh60-yasim.xml` is replaced by the generated
  `uh60a.xml` and `Systems/uh60a-aero.xml`, `-rotor.xml`, `-controls.xml`,
  `-drive.xml` and `-outputs.xml`. `Systems/controls.xml` sets the collective
  and power control levers' starting positions.
- **Nasal.** `Nasal/uh60.nas` is rewritten: the start and stop sequencer, the
  AFCS messages and the vibration of the view; `convert.nas` is no longer
  loaded.
- **Flight controls dialog** `Dialogs/afcs.xml`, with its menu entry in
  `Dialogs/uh60-menu.xml` and `Nasal/dialogs.nas`.
- **Instrument panel** laid out as in the operator's manual (figure 2-9):
  the instruments moved to their places and sized (`Panels/panel.xml`), a
  new face plate with its cut-outs (`panel2` in `Panels/panel.ac`) and its
  texture `Panels/panel-face.png` with the manual's placards. The torque
  dials, glass displays and moving maps, which a UH-60A doesn't have, are
  removed, and their Nasal (`map.nas`, `radar.nas`, `terrain-map.nas`,
  `zkv1000.nas`) is no longer loaded. `fdm/cockpit/panel_face.py` writes
  all of it.
- **New instruments** (`Panels/Instruments/`): vertical speed indicators
  (`vsi`, the airspeed indicator's case with the manual's dial), clocks
  (`clock`), stabilator position indicators (`stab`), the caution/advisory
  panel (`cap`, with `Systems/uh60a-cap.xml` and `Nasal/caution.nas`), the
  engine ignition switch (`ignition`), and the mode selectors, auxiliary
  fuel management panel, radar warning indicator, deice and IRCM panels and
  dimming controls (`modesel`, `cismode`, `afms`, `rwr`, `deice`, `nvgdim`,
  `radaltdim`), their faces from the manual's figure.
  `fdm/cockpit/instruments.py` and `cap.py` write them.
- **Cockpit instruments** calibrated to the new flight model: the pilot
  display unit (`prop-rpm`: % RPM of both engines and the rotor, % torque;
  its green band in `prop-rpm.png` lengthened to fill the whole scale), the
  central display unit (`combimeter`: Ng, oil, TGT and transmission per
  engine), the radar altimeter (`RadarAlt`, wheel height) and the master
  warning panel (`caution`, MASTER CAUTION from the caution/advisory panel).
- **Fixes** to bugs of the original aircraft: the cockpit collective lever
  moved with the passenger door instead of the collective
  (`sticks/collective.xml`), and the warning panel's thresholds were set for
  the old flight model (master caution always on).
- **Crew station** on the aircraft's butt lines (GenHel's cockpit
  geometry): the seats, pilots, cyclics and pedals on the seat centrelines
  at BL 24, the collectives at BL 11 (pilot) and BL 37 (copilot); they were
  about 7 cm further out (`Seats/all-seats.xml`, `Pilot/all-pilots.xml`,
  `sticks/all-sticks.xml`, `Pedals/all-pedals.xml`).
- **Views and sound.** The cockpit eye points at the design eye, BL 24 and
  WL 257, looking 21° down with a 90° field of view, so that the
  windshield's top frame stays just out of sight (`Systems/views.xml`); the
  pilots hidden from their own seat in
  FlightGear's and FGCamera's cockpit views (`Models/Pilot/all-pilots.xml`);
  a vibration rattle in `Sounds/uh60-sound.xml`.
- **Set file.** Description, a description for the launcher, credits and the
  flight model rating.

Everything else is as its authors made it; they are credited in
[UH-60A/Read-Me.txt](UH-60A/Read-Me.txt) and in the files themselves.
